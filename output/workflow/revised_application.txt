3. Project Summary
Community Wellness Alliance requests $50,000 for a 12-month Community Health Access
Initiative to help adults ages 45-75 better understand and access recommended preventive
health screenings. The project will combine accessible educational sessions, bilingual
informational materials where appropriate, and referral navigation to local healthcare
providers. Outreach will prioritize residents who report difficulty finding routine care,
understanding screening options, or scheduling follow-up visits.
Grant funds will support a part-time program coordinator, community outreach and education,
printed and digital materials, and basic monitoring of project activity. The initiative is designed
to connect participants with qualified healthcare professionals rather than deliver clinical
screening or medical advice directly. The organization will document activities and evaluate
engagement using privacy-conscious, aggregate measures.
4. Project Goals
The overall goal is to improve access to preventive healthcare information and referral
pathways for underserved adults ages 45-75. The proposed 12-month operational objectives
are:

5. Target Population
The initiative will focus on adults ages 45-75 in communities experiencing barriers to routine
preventive care, including limited access to a primary care provider, language barriers, cost
concerns, transportation challenges, and inflexible work schedules. Outreach materials will be
written in plain language, with translated versions developed according to the communities
served. Participation will be voluntary; no immigration, clinical, or other sensitive information
is required for general educational activities.
6. Project Activities
Months 1-2, preparation: Identify referral partners, confirm accessible community venues,
recruit and orient outreach staff, and develop educational materials for review by an
appropriately qualified health professional.
Months 3-10, implementation: Hold approximately one to two education sessions per month,
distribute outreach resources, and assist interested participants with locating clinics and
requesting appointments. Staff will explain logistical options without diagnosing conditions or
recommending individual treatment.
Months 11-12, assessment and reporting: Summarize session attendance, navigation
requests, participant feedback, and referral follow-up efforts. Review implementation barriers
with participating community partners and prepare a funder report.

8. Evaluation Plan
The project will use a simple process evaluation to determine whether planned activities
occurred and whether participants could access referral-navigation support. Staff will track the
number of sessions delivered, estimated unique participants reached, materials distributed,
navigation requests received, and documented follow-up attempts. Session attendance and
assistance records will be kept using minimal information necessary for operations.
The program coordinator will compile a monthly activity summary and review progress
toward the four proposed objectives. Participants may optionally complete a brief anonymous feedback questionnaire on clarity, usefulness, and remaining access barriers. As participant-reported outcomes, staff will summarize the number and proportion of respondents reporting that the information was clear and useful in the final report. Responses are voluntary and may not represent all participants; they do not verify screening completion. Staff will report
aggregate counts and clearly distinguish outreach or referral activity from verified clinical
outcomes. The program will not claim that participants completed screenings unless that result
can be lawfully and reliably verified.
At the end of 12 months, the organization will prepare a concise report comparing planned and
completed activities, describing implementation challenges, and recommending
improvements. Any collection, storage, or sharing of personal information must first be
reviewed against the organization's actual privacy and security policies.
